01
The foundation

Distance and similarity metrics

k-NN, k-means, hierarchical clustering, DBSCAN, kernels and t-SNE all start from the same question: how close are two points? The answer is a modelling choice. Change the metric and you change what “near”, “similar” and “cluster” mean. A true metric satisfies four rules: d(x, y) ≥ 0, d(x, y) = 0 only if x = y, symmetry, and the triangle inequality d(x, z) ≤ d(x, y) + d(y, z).

pMinkowski family

p = 1 is Manhattan, p = 2 Euclidean, p → ∞ Chebyshev. For p < 1 the triangle inequality breaks.

∠Cosine distance

Ignores length and compares only direction — the standard choice for text and embeddings.

ΣMahalanobis

Measures distance in units of the data’s own spread and correlation; Euclidean is the special case Σ = I.

Feature scaling changes who your neighbours are

Two features measured in different units — say age in years and income in thousands. Stretch one axis and the three nearest neighbours of the query (yellow lines) change completely. Standardising (z-scores) puts both features on equal footing.

Distance-based methods — k-NN, k-means, SVM with RBF kernel, DBSCAN, PCA — are all sensitive to units. Trees are not: a split “x₂ ≤ t” works in any unit.

02
Centroids

k-means clustering

k-means alternates two simple steps until nothing changes: assign every point to its nearest centroid, then move each centroid to the mean of its points. Each step can only lower the total squared distance (inertia), so it always converges — but only to a local minimum, which is why the starting positions matter and why k-means++ seeding was invented.

JObjective (inertia)

The sum of squared distances from each point to its centroid.

⇄Lloyd’s two steps

Assignment is a Voronoi partition; the update is a mean (median for L1, normalised mean for cosine).

++k-means++ seeding

Pick the next centre with probability proportional to its squared distance from the centres chosen so far.

03
Merge by merge

Hierarchical (agglomerative) clustering

Start with every point as its own cluster and repeatedly merge the two closest clusters until one remains. The full history is a tree — the dendrogram — and cutting it at any height gives a clustering, so you never have to fix k in advance. What “closest clusters” means is the linkage, and it changes everything.

⟷Single & complete

Single: the closest pair (finds chains, suffers from “chaining”). Complete: the farthest pair (compact clusters).

μAverage linkage

The mean of all pairwise distances (UPGMA) — a compromise between the two.

WWard

Merge the pair that increases the total within-cluster variance the least — the hierarchical cousin of k-means.

04
Clusters are dense regions

DBSCAN: density-based clustering

DBSCAN needs no k. A point is a core point if at least minPts points lie within distance ε of it. Clusters grow by hopping from core point to core point; points reached but not dense themselves are border points, and everything else is noise. It finds clusters of any shape and flags outliers — but one ε cannot fit clusters of very different density.

05
Soft clustering

Gaussian mixture models and EM

A Gaussian mixture assumes the data were generated by k Gaussian “bumps”, each with its own mean, covariance and weight. The EM algorithm fits them by alternating an E-step (how responsible is each component for each point?) and an M-step (refit each Gaussian to the points it is responsible for). Unlike k-means, every point belongs to every cluster with some probability, and clusters can be elongated ellipses.

pMixture density

A weighted sum of Gaussians; the weights πₖ sum to one.

EE-step: responsibilities

The posterior probability that component k generated point i.

MM-step: refit

Weighted means and covariances. The log-likelihood never decreases.

06
Fewer dimensions, same story

Dimensionality reduction: PCA and t-SNE

PCA finds the directions along which the data vary the most and projects onto them: a linear, global method with a closed-form answer. t-SNE instead tries to keep each point’s neighbours close in 2-D, which lets it unfold curved manifolds — at the price of distorting global distances and depending on its perplexity setting.

ΣPCA = eigenvectors of Σ

The first principal component maximises the projected variance.

%Explained variance

Each component keeps a share λₖ / Σλ of the total variance.

KLt-SNE objective

Match neighbour probabilities in high-D (Gaussian) and 2-D (heavy-tailed Student-t).

Clustering arena

Eight algorithms, one dataset. Because the data were generated, we know the true groups and can score each result with the adjusted Rand index (ARI: 1 = perfect, 0 = no better than chance). The silhouette needs no ground truth — but notice how it rewards round, k-means-like blobs even when the true clusters are rings or moons.
